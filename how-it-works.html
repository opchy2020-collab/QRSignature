How it works

From a link to a finished code in a minute, with nothing sent to anyone.

The short version

  1. Choose a type

    Website link, Wi-Fi, contact card, email, phone, text message, WhatsApp chat, social profile or plain text. The form changes to match.

  2. Design it

    Start from a template, then change the dot pattern, corner shapes, colors, border and logo. The preview updates at once.

  3. Check it

    The scan check reads your design the way a phone would and lists what is good and what to fix.

  4. Save and share

    Download a PNG or SVG, copy the image, or share it to Facebook, WhatsApp, LinkedIn, X or Instagram.

What the scan check tests

The check runs on your device, on the exact design you see. It gives a verdict of Scans well, Scans, with notes or May not scan, and a list of reasons.

  • Contrast. The dots must stand out from the background. Pale dots on a pale background is the most common reason a code fails.
  • Light on dark. A light code on a dark background works on most new phones, but some older scanners cannot read it.
  • Empty border. A code needs a quiet margin of about four small squares around it so a scanner can find its edges.
  • Logo size. A logo hides part of the pattern. The code makes up for it with extra error correction, but a very large logo is still a risk.
  • Data density. The more you put in a code, the finer its squares. The check tells you the smallest width to print.
  • Transparent background. The code will take the color of whatever it is placed on, so make sure that color is plain and light.
Test it on a real phone. The check is a careful guide, not a promise. Before you print a hundred flyers, scan one sample with two different phones.

Good habits for printing

  • Use the SVG download for print. It stays sharp at any size.
  • Print on a plain, flat surface. Curved or shiny surfaces can hide parts of the code.
  • Keep dark dots on a light background whenever you can.
  • Do not squash or stretch the code. It must stay a perfect square.
  • Say what the code does, for example “Scan to see the menu”.

About sharing

The Facebook, WhatsApp, LinkedIn and X buttons open each network's own share screen in a new tab. Nothing is posted until you confirm there. Facebook and LinkedIn share a web address, so they are available for website links and social profiles.

Instagram does not offer a share link for websites. On a phone, the Instagram button opens your phone's share sheet with the image. On a computer, it saves the image so you can post it from your pictures.
